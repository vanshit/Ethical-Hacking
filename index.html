Same tools, same techniques as attackers — used with permission, to make systems safer.

Interactive · Scenario Quiz 1/2

Allowed… or Not Allowed?

01
Scanning your own lab VM.
Allowed. You own the system, so authorization is implicit. Your own lab is exactly where sharp tools should be practiced.
02
Scanning a company's public IP without authorization.
Not allowed. "Public" ≠ "permission". Scanning without written authorization is unauthorized activity — and illegal under most computer-misuse laws.
03
Testing a bug-bounty target within its published scope.
Allowed. The program grants explicit, documented permission — and the scope defines exactly what you may test. Stay inside it.

Interactive · Scenario Quiz 2/2

Allowed… or Not Allowed?

04
Exploiting a production system that's outside the authorized scope.
Not allowed. Beyond scope = unauthorized, even if you're "sure" it's vulnerable. Discovering a flaw doesn't grant the right to touch it — report it instead.
05
Testing a vulnerable machine intentionally provided by the instructor.
Allowed. Explicit permission from the instructor, and the environment was built to be exploited. This is authorized testing in its purest form.
⛨
The Pattern

Every "allowed" answer had explicit authorization behind it. When in doubt: ask first, get it in writing.

The CIA Triad

C

every security decision is a trade-off between these three — next slides break each one apart

CIA Triad · 1 of 3

Confidentiality — Keep Secrets Secret

C
Definition

Data is visible and accessible only to authorized people and systems — nothing more, nothing less.

⚔ How It Breaks

  • Credential theft — phishing, stuffing, keyloggers
  • Data leakage — exposed buckets, misconfigured APIs
  • Unauthorized database access — broken access control

⛨ How It's Defended

  • Encryption — at rest & in transit
  • Access control — least privilege, RBAC
  • MFA — credentials alone stop being enough
Real-World ExampleA breach exposes customer PII — names, emails, payment history surface on the dark web.
Business ImpactRegulatory fines, lawsuits, lost customer trust — usually costing far more than the fix would have.

CIA Triad · 2 of 3

Integrity — Trust the Data

I
Definition

Data is accurate, complete, and protected from unauthorized modification — you can trust what you read.

⚔ How It Breaks

  • Web defacement — content replaced without consent
  • Database manipulation — silent record tampering
  • Supply-chain tampering — trusted updates turned malicious

⛨ How It's Defended

  • Hashing & signatures — tamper-evident data
  • Change monitoring — file integrity, audit trails
  • Signed releases — verified code pipelines
Real-World ExampleAccount balances silently altered, or a homepage defaced — nobody knows which records to trust anymore.
Business ImpactBad decisions from corrupted data; every record becomes suspect until proven otherwise.

CIA Triad · 3 of 3

Availability — Up When Needed

A
Definition

Systems, data, and services are accessible and functional when authorized users need them.

⚔ How It Breaks

  • DDoS — traffic floods drown the service
  • Ransomware — your own files held hostage
  • Resource exhaustion — disk, CPU, memory drained

⛨ How It's Defended

  • Redundancy & failover — no single point of failure
  • Tested backups — restore drills, not just copies
  • DDoS protection — scrubbing, rate limiting
Real-World ExampleRansomware takes a hospital's systems offline — appointments, scans, and records grind to a halt.
Business ImpactRevenue stops by the minute; for critical services, downtime can be life-threatening.

    Module 4 · Technique 03

    Vishing: When the Attack Talks Back

    The Voice Playbook

    • Voice phishing — the scam arrives as a call, not an email
    • Caller-ID spoofing — caller ID is a label the network carries, not proof of identity; it can show your bank's real number while a stranger holds the phone
    • Fake support calls — "we detected a problem on your device, install this…"
    • Fake banking calls — "read us the OTP to freeze your account"
    • Executive impersonation — a "director" voice pushing one urgent payment

    Why Voice Hits Differently

    • Real-time pressure — no pause button; answers are demanded now
    • No headers to inspect — you can't right-click a conversation
    • Familiarity bias — a voice that "sounds right" feels like proof
    • Cloning era — seconds of audio can now reproduce a familiar voice
    • The defense — hang up, then call back on a number you already trust
    ⚠
    The Golden Rule of Calls

    Whoever calls you controls the pace. Ending the call costs nothing — you can always dial the official number back and continue safely.

    Synthetic People, Real Damage

    Deepfake Social Engineering

    The Techniques

    • Face synthesis — photorealistic faces that don't exist
    • Voice cloning — seconds of audio become anyone's voice
    • Lip-sync manipulation — real video, new words
    • Synthetic identities — personas built from generated media
    • Executive impersonation — the CFO on the call isn't the CFO
    • Fraud scenarios — urgency + familiarity + a fake face

    The Controls

    • Out-of-band verification — confirm on a second channel
    • MFA — identity that survives a faked voice
    • Transaction approval workflows — no single-human transfers
    • Call-back procedures — ring the known number back
    • Identity verification & anti-fraud controls
    • Media provenance (C2PA-style) + security awareness
    ⚠
    The Human Heuristic

    Urgent + unusual + unfamiliar channel = verify. That rule defeats most deepfake social engineering — no technology required.

    Section F · Knowledge Checks

    PROVE YOUR JUDGMENT

    Five quizzes and two scenarios. Every answer gets an instant verdict, the reasoning behind it, and one takeaway to carry into the lab.

    Quiz 1 of 7 · The Ethics Line

    Is This Ethical Hacking?

    ▣ Scenario QuestionA friend tells you: "I found a company's admin panel with no password and looked around for fun. I didn't break anything or steal anything — so I'm basically doing ethical hacking, right?"
    ⓘ
    Why

    Ethical hacking is defined by authorization, not by intent or outcome. Accessing a system without permission is unauthorized access — illegal under computer-misuse laws (in India, IT Act §43/§66) even when nothing is broken, copied, or stolen. "Just looking" is still access.

    ⛨
    Key Takeaway

    Permission before packets. No written authorization = not ethical hacking, however harmless it feels.

    Quiz 2 of 7 · The CIA Triad

    Which CIA Property Is Affected?

    ▣ Scenario QuestionDuring exam week, attackers flood the college results portal with junk traffic until it collapses. Students can't log in to view their marks. Nobody read or modified any data — it's simply unreachable.
    ⓘ
    Why

    No data was exposed (so not confidentiality) and none was altered (so not integrity). The attack made the service unreachable to the people entitled to use it — the textbook definition of an availability failure. A DDoS is the classic example.

    ⛨
    Key Takeaway

    Ask three questions: was it seen (C), changed (I), or made unreachable (A)? The answer names the property under attack.

    Quiz 3 of 7 · The Team Model

    Red Team, Blue Team… or Purple?

    ▣ Scenario QuestionAfter an approved phishing simulation, the "attackers" and "defenders" sit in the same room, replay every alert together, and jointly write two new detection rules based on what they learned. Which team model is this?
    ⓘ
    Why

    Red simulates attackers. Blue builds defenses. The moment they share findings and improve defenses together — replay, tune, retest — that joint feedback loop is exactly what "purple" means. It isn't a third separate discipline; it's red and blue collaborating.

    ⛨
    Key Takeaway

    Attack teaches, defense learns, purple connects them. If both sides improve together from one exercise, you just watched purple teaming.

    Quiz 4 of 7 · Bug-Bounty Scope

    Inside Scope… or Outside?

    ▣ Scenario QuestionA company's bug-bounty program lists only shop.company.com. While testing it, you discover an interesting endpoint on api.company.com — same company, not on the list. You probe it "just a little". Is this action inside scope?
    ⓘ
    Why

    A bug-bounty program is a written grant of permission — it authorizes exactly what it lists, nothing more. Same company ≠ same scope, and severity never expands authorization. Testing an unlisted asset is unauthorized access. The right move: stop, then ask the program to confirm or extend scope.

    ⛨
    Key Takeaway

    If it isn't listed, it's forbidden. When tempted to go beyond scope — stop and ask. That habit is what separates researchers from offenders.

    Quiz 5 of 7 · Synthetic Media

    Deepfake… or Legitimate?

    ▣ Scenario QuestionYou get a video call from your CFO asking you to urgently approve an unusual payment — "it's confidential, don't discuss it with the team." The face and voice seem right, but the lip movements look slightly off. What is the safest judgment?
    ⓘ
    Why

    Live video no longer proves identity — faces and voices can be synthesized in real time. The pressure pattern (urgent + confidential + bypasses normal approvals) is the classic fraud signature, exactly as in the Arup case where a deepfake "CFO" call cost HK$200M. Call back on a known number and route it through the transaction approval workflow.

    ⛨
    Key Takeaway

    Seeing is not believing. Urgent + unusual + "keep it secret" → verify on a second channel before any payment.

    AI Scenario 7 of 7 · The Voice Message

    The CEO's Voice Message

    ▣ Scenario QuestionYou receive a WhatsApp voice message, apparently from your CEO, requesting an urgent ₹2 lakh payment to a new vendor today. The voice sounds exactly like him. What should you verify before anything else?
    ⓘ
    Why

    A convincing voice needs only seconds of sample audio to clone — hearing is no longer proof. Confirming inside the same chat can reach the same attacker, and group opinions can't reliably detect clones. The controls that work: out-of-band verification (call a known number), MFA and transaction approval workflows, plus reporting the attempt to security. This exact pattern drove the Indore voice-clone fraud.

    ⛨
    Key Takeaway

    Urgency + money + familiar voice = verify on a separate channel. Every payment request follows the approval workflow — no exceptions for "the CEO".

    The Undelivered Parcel

    MAILFORENSICS v3.2 · CASE #001EASY
    Where to look:sender domainreply-toauthentication resultsthe linkthe ask
    ▣ Message Headers
    From:"SpeedEx Courier" <noreply@speedex-delivery-update.net>
    Reply-To:[email protected]
    Subject:FINAL NOTICE: Parcel #SPX-88231 could not be delivered
    Date:Tue, 10:14 IST
    Auth-Results:spf=fail smtp.mailfrom=speedex-delivery-update.net; dkim=none; dmarc=none
    ▣ Message Body

    Dear Customer,
    Our courier attempted delivery of your parcel #SPX-88231 twice, but no one was available. To avoid the parcel being returned to sender, please pay a small re-delivery fee of ₹2 within the next 24 hours:

    track-your-parcel.speedex-delivery-update.net/redelivery

    SpeedEx Customer Team
    This is an automated message. Do not reply.

    ▣ Your Verdict
    ⚠ VERDICT: PHISHING CONFIRMED · COURIER-FEE SCAM (SMISHING PATTERN)
    IOC-01Look-alike sender domain. Real couriers send from one domain. speedex-delivery-update.net stacks hyphenated words to imitate a brand nobody can verify in a glance.
    IOC-02Reply-To ≠ From. Any reply would route to a second unknown domain — a classic split identity used to dodge sender checks.
    IOC-03spf=fail · dkim=none · dmarc=none. The sending server is not authorized to mail as that domain — the sender address is effectively forged.
    IOC-04"Dear Customer" with no real tracking. A genuine courier names you and links its real tracking site; mass-produced lures stay generic.
    IOC-05₹2 fee + 24-hour timer. Tiny amounts slip past suspicion; urgency stops you checking. The fee is bait for your card details.
    🧠
    Why it works

    Fear of losing a parcel + sunk waiting time. Victims think "it's only ₹2" — but the payment form harvests the card.

    ⛨
    The defense

    Never pay through an email/SMS link. Track on the courier's official app or site, report the message, delete it.

    +20 XP · attack indicators identifiedPATTERN: fee + urgency + look-alike domain

    From:"HR Desk" <hr-notices@corp-hr-benefits.com>
    Reply-To:[email protected]

    ▣ Your Verdict
    ⚠ VERDICT: PHISHING CONFIRMED · PAYROLL DIVERSION (BANK-DETAIL HARVEST)
    IOC-01"Internal" HR mailing from an external domain. Your HR writes from yourcompany.com — never from a generic corp-hr-benefits.com that imitates corporate-speak.
    IOC-02spf=fail · dkim=none · dmarc=fail. All three authentication checks reject this sender.
    IOC-03undisclosed-recipients. Real payroll mail is personal and individual — mass BCC betrays a mass lure.
    IOC-04Bank details requested through a web link. Legitimate banks/HR collect account changes inside authenticated portals or on paper — never via emailed links.
    IOC-0548-hour deadline tied to your salary. Fear of losing money you've already earned is the pressure lever.
    🧠
    Why it works

    Salary touches everyone at once — greed plus fear plus a plausible process. Victims imagine the worst (delayed pay) instead of checking the domain.

    ⛨
    The defense

    Verify with HR on a known channel or the official HR portal. Never submit bank details through an email link; report it to security.

    MAILFORENSICS v3.2 · CASE #003MEDIUM

    ▣ Message Headers
    From:"Ananya Sharma" <a.sharma@cornpany-finance.com>
    Subject:Q3 Bonus Sheet — preview before Friday (confidential)
    Attachment:Q3_Bonus_Sheet.xlsm (42 KB)
    Auth-Results:spf=pass smtp.mailfrom=cornpany-finance.com; dkim=pass; dmarc=pass
    ▣ Message Body

    Sharing the Q3 bonus sheet before Friday's announcement — you're on the list 😉 It opens in protected view, so just click "Enable Content" to see the numbers.

    Keep it between us for now.

    — Ananya

    ▣ Your Verdict
    ⚠ VERDICT: PHISHING CONFIRMED · MALICIOUS ATTACHMENT (TYPO-SQUATTED SENDER)
    IOC-01cornpany-finance.com — a typo-squat. One letter swapped: cornpany instead of company. At a glance it reads right; letter-by-letter it's a fake.
    IOC-02SPF/DKIM/DMARC all pass — and that's the lesson. Those checks only prove the mail came from the domain it claims. Here the claimed domain is the attacker's own, so of course it passes. Green checks ≠ safe sender.
    IOC-03.xlsm = macro-enabled workbook. Plain data doesn't need macros. "Enable Content" is the moment the payload runs.
    IOC-04Curiosity + secrecy hooks. "You're on the list" pulls you in; "keep it between us" stops you asking anyone to verify.
    IOC-05Internal data arriving by external email. Real bonus sheets live on the intranet/HR portal — not in attachments from unknown domains.
    🧠
    Why it works

    Money + gossip + exclusivity is an almost irresistible mix, and the passing header checks disarm the careful ones.

    ⛨
    The defense

    Never enable macros on unexpected files. Verify with "Ananya" on an internal channel; report the mail to security.

    +30 XP · attack mechanism explainedPATTERN: typo-squat + macro file + secrecy

    The Trusted Colleague

    MAILFORENSICS v3.2 · CASE #004EXPERT

    ▣ Message Headers
    From:Priya Nair <[email protected]> ← real internal address
    Reply-To:vpn-helpdesk@yourcompany-portal-support.com
    Subject:Re: Re: VPN access renewal
    Received:from mail.yourcompany.com · Tue 17:42 IST
    Auth-Results:spf=pass smtp.mailfrom=yourcompany.com; dkim=pass header.d=yourcompany.com; dmarc=pass
    ▣ Message Body

    Hey — following up on the VPN thread.

    IT moved renewals to the new portal. Please re-authenticate with your current password before 6 pm today or your access lapses until next week:

    https://vpn.yourcompany-portal-support.com/renew

    — Priya

    ▣ Your Verdict
    ⚠ VERDICT: PHISHING CONFIRMED · COMPROMISED ACCOUNT + THREAD HIJACK
    NOTESPF, DKIM and DMARC all pass — because they should. The mail really came from Priya's real account… which the attacker has compromised. Authentication proves origin, not intent.
    IOC-01Reply-To diverts to yourcompany-portal-support.com. A hyphen-suffixed look-alike of your domain. Any reply lands with the attacker, not Priya.
    IOC-02Read the link's real domain. Not yourcompany.com — the actual host is yourcompany-portal-support.com, dressed to skim past a glance.
    IOC-03"Re-authenticate with your current password." No real SSO/VPN flow asks you to type your current password into an emailed link. That ask is the credential harvest.
    IOC-0417:42 send + "before 6 pm" deadline. Odd timing plus a tight window — pressure instead of process. Real IT renewals come with weeks of notice.
    🧠
    Why it works

    The most trusted disguise is a real colleague inside a real thread. Victims check the name, not the domain — and green checkmarks finish the job.

    ⛨
    The defense

    Ping Priya on internal chat, report to the SOC immediately (her account is burning), never enter the password. If clicked: rotate credentials + review MFA sessions.
